If you have just finished Class 12 in Lucknow and are looking at BBA, BCA and
B.Com, you are choosing between three degrees that look similar on paper. All
three run for three years. All three are open to students from any 10+2 stream.
At City College of Management, Chinhat, all three are affiliated to the
University of Lucknow. The difference is what you study, what you can do with
it, and which kind of work you enjoy.

<h2 id="teaches">1. What each course actually teaches</h2>

**BBA (Bachelor of Business Administration)** is about how organisations are
run. The syllabus covers principles of management, marketing, finance, human
resources, accounting, business law, economics and entrepreneurship. Teaching
at CCM uses case studies, presentations, group projects and industry visits, so
much of the learning happens through discussion rather than memorising.

**BCA (Bachelor of Computer Applications)** is about building software. It
covers programming, data structures, database management, operating systems,
computer networks, web technologies and software engineering. Students spend
regular hours in the computer lab on programming assignments, and finish with a
final-year project.

**B.Com (Bachelor of Commerce)** is about accounts, tax and finance in depth.
It covers financial accounting, corporate accounting, cost accounting, income
tax, GST, business law, banking, economics and business statistics, with
practical work on accounting software [e.g. Tally].

<h2 id="suits">2. Which one suits which student</h2>

Choose **BBA** if you enjoy working with people, speaking in front of a group,
and organising things, and if you are thinking about an MBA later.

Choose **BCA** if you like solving puzzles, do not mind sitting with a problem
until the code works, and want to enter the IT industry directly.

Choose **B.Com** if you are comfortable with numbers and rules, and want the
strongest base for CA, CS, CMA or a banking career.

None of the three requires a particular 10+2 stream. Science and arts students
take all three every year. For BCA, check whether Mathematics is required in
the current university norms [confirm Maths requirement].

<h2 id="careers">3. Careers after each degree</h2>

| Degree | Typical first roles |
| --- | --- |
| BBA | Business development executive, marketing and sales executive, HR executive, operations executive, banking and financial services roles |
| BCA | Software developer, web developer, database assistant, IT support engineer, system administrator, testing and technical content roles |
| B.Com | Accountant, tax assistant, audit assistant, banking associate, insurance and finance roles, government examinations |

The Training & Placement Cell at CCM trains students in communication,
aptitude, group discussion and interviews, and runs campus drives, including
pool drives with City Group of Colleges. See [placements](/placements/).

<h2 id="higher">4. Higher study options</h2>

After BBA: MBA or PGDM. After BCA: MCA, or an MBA in IT. After B.Com: M.Com,
MBA, or the professional routes — CA, CS and CMA, which many B.Com students
prepare for alongside their degree, with faculty guidance.

<h2 id="decide">6. How to decide</h2>

Ask yourself which of these three days you would rather have: running a team
meeting and presenting a plan (BBA), debugging a program until it runs (BCA), or
closing a set of accounts correctly (B.Com). That answer is usually more
reliable than any salary comparison.
